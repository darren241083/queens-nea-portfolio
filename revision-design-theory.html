Chapter 01  /  3.2.2 Design theory
01 · Design influences, styles & movements

Four ways of thinking

Arts and Crafts, Art Deco, Modernism & Bauhaus, Postmodernism & Memphis. Each is a set of principles — the look is the result.

Can you recognise a movement just by how it looks — and is that enough for the exam?
From the specNo. A movement should be discussed through its principles, not only through recognition of a visual style. For example, Bauhaus is connected with industrial production and social ideas as well as simple geometry.

03 · Socio-economic influences

The world shapes the product

After the First World War, during the Second World War, and in today’s consumer culture — each period put different pressures on designers.

Why would a war change the way a chair is designed?
Second World WarWartime shortages required controlled use of materials, simplified construction and prioritisation of essential function. Utility approaches cut decorative excess and standardised designs so scarce resources could serve wider populations.

05b  /  Exam technique

Apply, don’t list

A list of six words earns little. Flip the switch to see the upgrade.
Showing: the weak answer
Lists without applying

“The designer used the six Rs: rethink, refuse, reduce, reuse, repair and recycle.”

Recall only. Nothing links a strategy to the product.

Chooses the most significant R and explains it

“For this product, repair is the most significant strategy: making faults diagnosable and parts replaceable extends service life, so fewer new products need to be made. Reduce also matters because using less material and energy prevents resource use before recycling is even needed.”

Explains which strategies are most significant for the particular product, as the specification asks.

Q1 · Same idea, twice?

This student wrote four points. How many marks do they get?
✗

What NOT to award

Do not award separate marks for two statements that communicate the same characteristic.

How many marks?
  • 1Postmodern products borrow features from older design styles.✓ 1 mark
  • 2They mix together features from different historical periods.✗ 0Same characteristic as point 1 — combining or borrowing earlier styles.
  • 3Form may be more important than practical function.✓ 1 mark
  • 4How it looks matters more than how well it works.✗ 0Same characteristic as point 3 — form prioritised over function.
Fix it: check your four points are four different ideas — e.g. swap point 2 for “products may be treated as art or statement pieces” and point 4 for “products may communicate political or social messages”.

Q2 · Bauhaus and manufacturing

What would you give it?

The question · 4 parts to answer

Explain how the use of tubular steel and laminated veneers helped Bauhaus designers create products suitable for mass production. You should refer to at least one specific product in your answer.

6 marks
Model answer

Tubular steel helped Bauhaus designers make light but strong furniture frames. It could be bent into simple shapes without needing carved decoration, so the appearance matched the Bauhaus idea of form follows function. Chrome plating also gave products a consistent machine-made finish. This can be seen in the Wassily chair, where the tubular frame is part of the appearance as well as the structure.

Laminated veneers could be formed over moulds to produce the same curved component repeatedly. This reduced waste and made it easier to manufacture identical parts in larger quantities. Both methods therefore helped Bauhaus designers create simple, standardised products that could be made more cheaply for ordinary people.

Q2 · Describe ≠ explain

Three answers that fall short of the top band. Choose a band for each, then reveal what the examiner would not credit.
✗

What NOT to award

Answers that mainly describe the materials without explaining their influence stay in the 1–2 band. Explaining only one material caps the answer at 3–4.

Answer A

“Bauhaus chairs look modern and nice.”

Band:
0 marks
  • Nothing worthy of credit — an opinion, not knowledge.
  • No material, process or product mentioned.
Answer B

“Bauhaus designers used tubular steel and plywood. Tubular steel is shiny and strong. Plywood is made of layers.”

Band:
1–2 marks
  • Basic statements about Bauhaus, tubular steel or plywood.
  • Mainly describes the materials — never explains their influence.
  • No connection to mass production or design principles.
  • No named product.
Answer C

“Tubular steel could be bent into simple frames, so the same frame could be made repeatedly in a factory. An example is the Wassily chair.”

Band:
3–4 marks
  • Explains one material and links it to production.
  • Laminated veneers are missing — the top band needs both.
  • Example is named but not developed — what does the Wassily chair show?

Q3 · Charles & Ray Eames

The question · 3 parts to answer

Using specific product examples, outline how the work of Charles and Ray Eames represented Modernist principles and influenced later furniture design.

6 marks
Model answer

Charles and Ray Eames used modern materials and processes to make furniture with simple, functional forms. Their LCW and DCW chairs used moulded plywood, which meant layers of veneer could be shaped into repeatable curved parts instead of using heavy carved timber. This reflects Modernism because the manufacturing process and function helped determine the shape.

They also developed single-piece fibreglass chair shells that could be reproduced using moulds and made in bold colours. Their work influenced later designers such as Verner Panton and can also be seen in simple, mass-produced furniture sold by companies such as IKEA. Their main impact was showing that modern furniture could be attractive, functional and suitable for repeated manufacture.

06g  /  Q3 what not to award

Q3 · Naming ≠ explaining

Choose a band for each answer, then reveal what the examiner would not credit.
✗

What NOT to award

Naming a product or material without explaining its importance is 1–2 mark work. Generic knowledge of “famous designers” earns little.

Answer A

“Charles and Ray Eames designed the LCW chair. It is made of plywood. They were very famous designers.”

Band:
1–2 marks
  • Names a relevant product and material.
  • Names without explaining why moulded plywood mattered (repeatable curved parts).
  • “Very famous” is generic — no credit.
  • No connection to Modernism or later design.
Answer B

“The Eameses used moulded plywood so curved parts could be made again and again. This was very modern. Other designers copied them.”

Band:
3–4 marks
  • Sound knowledge of a manufacturing method and why it helped.
  • “Very modern” is not a Modernist principle — say how: process and function determined the form.
  • “Other designers copied them” — which designers? Name Verner Panton’s S Chair, flat-pack furniture or IKEA.
  • No specific product named.
